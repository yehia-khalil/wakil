The exit criterion: a playing video floats into the system's picture-in-picture window, with no
click, when you leave it — a new tab, another pane, another place, a minimised window — and
**Restore** brings you back to its organisation, place, pane and tab. A paused or finished video
never floats. Settings → Browser turns it off, and says why when WebKit can't.

## 0. Setup

Run this in the worktree root, on this Mac. It is safe to re-run.

```bash
make build && make app-bundle
WAKIL_ORG=personal app/Wakil.app/Contents/MacOS/Wakil
```

Rows 1 to 3 and 5 to 10 need one project open with a browser pane. Row 4 needs a second environment
and a second project. Row 8 needs a page with an embedded YouTube player.

## 2. Known and not fixed

- A `<video>` inside a shadow root is not found, so it does not float.
- ⌘H does not float: hiding the app is not a window change WebKit or Wakil sees.
- The Restore dock (a view with no window, kept below its siblings and invisible) is proven in plain
  AppKit. In the real window, over SwiftUI's hosting view, rows 2 and 4 are the check.
- Maximise follows focus: focusing a pane changes which maximised pane is in front, so a video in a
  pane that becomes covered floats, and one uncovered comes back. Being covered is leaving (ruling 1),
  though focus caused it.
- A pane rebuilt by a layout change may float briefly and come back. Unverified.
- Restore, Close, drag and resize belong to the system's window. Only the walk can reach them.
- The page's own muted or silent video is paused by WebKit when its view leaves the window. Wakil
  floats it anyway if it was playing when the page went hidden; a video you paused yourself does not
  (probe h, spec amendment).
- Floating needs two WebKit private callbacks and one private preference. If a macOS update drops
  one, the setting turns itself off and says which is missing; nothing floats.
